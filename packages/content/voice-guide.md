# Voice guide

Status: **draft for the owner's approval.** Nothing is drafted from this until the owner has read it and the twelve meaning sheets, per Gate 1 of the plan. Every snippet in the bank derives from the meaning sheets and follows this page.

## What the writing is for

Numerology here is a symbolic tradition, offered for reflection and fun. The numbers underneath carry very little information, so the writing does not pretend to know anything about the reader. It names a theme, shows how that theme tends to look, and asks a question the reader can answer for themselves. That is the point of view of the product.

## Rules

1. **Second person, plain words.** Write to "you". Prefer short sentences and everyday vocabulary. Aim for a reading grade under 10.
2. **Hedge honestly.** Write "tends to", "may", "you may notice", "often", "can". The lint blocks certainty words (will, always, never, must, guaranteed, destined) and rewrites are done by hand.
3. **Name patterns, not events.** Describe habits, attention, energy and ways of relating. Never say what is going to happen to someone.
4. **Shadow side without fear.** Every number has a shadow. Describe it in neutral, specific terms ("you may hold on to control") and follow it with something the reader can try. No curses, no doom, no bad luck.
5. **End on a prompt.** Every reading ends on a reflection question, not a verdict. The final section of a number reading, a year, a month and a day is a question.
6. **Masters are themes, not ranks.** Always give the root next to the master ("11/2"). Do not say a master number makes someone special, gifted or chosen.
7. **No claims the numbers cannot carry.** The site never predicts: illness, death, pregnancy, accidents; money moves (buying, selling, investing, quitting); legal outcomes; relationship verdicts (staying, leaving, marrying). The lint blocks the words that usually introduce them. Write about effort, attention and how a day is spent instead.
8. **Do not flatter.** Specific-sounding text from few inputs is what the Forer effect runs on. Prefer observations with a cost as well as a benefit.
9. **British or American spelling?** American, to match the interface.

## Layer by layer

- **Number readings (life layer).** Five sections: overview, strengths, shadow, relationships, growth practice. The same number reads differently by place: *life path* is the road and its recurring lessons; *expression* is what you bring and how you work; *soul urge* is what you want underneath; *personality* is how others tend to meet you first; *birth day* is a knack from the day itself; *maturity* is where you grow toward later. Growth ends on a question.
- **Year.** Six sections: overview, work, relationships, inner life, watch for, reflect. Reflect is a question. Year × life path lines are one or two sentences that begin after "For your life path ...".
- **Month.** Three sections: theme, focus, reflect. Month × year lines say how a month sits inside its year.
- **Day.** A headline (one line, an invitation not a forecast), then six facets: work, relationships, mind, one small action, watch for, reflect. Facets are one or two short sentences. The reflect facet is a question. Day × month lines are clauses that continue "Inside a ... month 7 of a ... year 6, ...". Day × life path lines continue "For your life path 1: ...".
- **Special dates.** A short note on what the date is and one way to use it as a prompt. No folklore about luck.

## Approved examples

> **Day 8 headline.** Finish what you started today instead of starting more.

> **Day 8, watch for.** Carrying other people's load because saying no feels harder.

> **Day 8, reflect.** What would "enough" look like today?

> **Life path 8, shadow.** Control and overwork are the usual costs. You may measure a day by what it produced, and rest can start to feel like falling behind. Notice when the effort stops serving the thing you set out to build.

## Banned and flagged (enforced by the lint)

Certainty: will, won't, always, never, must, guaranteed, definitely, certainly, destined, fated.
Fear hooks: cursed, doomed, disaster, tragic, ruin, unlucky, bad luck, beware, dangerous.
Health and body: illness, sick, disease, death, die, pregnant, accident, injury, surgery, health, medical, doctor.
Money moves: buy, sell, invest, quit, resign, purchase, lottery, gamble, stock, loan.
Legal: lawsuit, court, sue, verdict, legal, lawyer.
Relationship verdicts: leave, leaving, marry, marriage, divorce, break up, cheat, affair, soulmate.

## Between us (addendum, draft for the owner's approval)

Between us sets two people's numbers side by side. It follows every rule above, and adds these. The notes for each number pair are in `meaning-sheets/pairs.md`.

1. **Write about numbers, not people.** "A 3 and a 7", "the two of you", "each of you". Never "your partner is...". The other person did not agree to be described.
2. **Read right for any two people.** No partner, husband, wife, boyfriend, girlfriend, spouse or lover. No "he", "she", "him" or "her". The same text has to work for two friends, a parent and child, or two colleagues.
3. **No scores and no verdicts.** No percentage, rating, ranking or "match". No "compatible" or "incompatible", "soulmate", "perfect" or "ideal". Never say whether two people should begin, stay or part.
4. **Differences are where each may stretch.** Not problems, not red flags. Say what each side could try.
5. **Questions each person answers about themselves.** "What do you need before...?" never "Why do you always...?". A pair's text ends on a question.
6. **The text reads the same in either order.** Never "the first" or "the second" person; the page shows who is who.
7. **A master number is a theme.** It adds one short note. It never makes a pair special, rarer or better.
8. **Two fixed safety lines** show on every Between us screen, outside the bank: "Numbers can't tell you whether to begin, stay or leave. That is for you to decide." and "If you ever feel unsafe with someone, talk to a person you trust or a local support service."

Banned only here (enforced by the lint): compatible, incompatible, match, soulmate, twin flame, meant to be, destiny, perfect, ideal, toxic, red flag, score, rating, rank, percent; partner, husband, wife, boyfriend, girlfriend, spouse, lover, romantic; he, she, him, her, his; narcissist, abusive, manipulative; attract, in love, fall for.

## Disclaimer

Shown on onboarding, under every reading and on the PDF, word for word:

> Numerology is a symbolic tradition. There is no scientific evidence that it predicts events. Use it for reflection or fun, not for decisions about health, money, legal matters or relationships.

## The Chaldean tradition and the shadows (addendum, draft for the owner's approval)

The site starts in the Chaldean tradition. Everything above applies, and these rules add to it. The plan is in `docs/chaldean-and-shadows.md`.

### Compound numbers (10 to 52)

1. **Three parts.** What the tradition says, the shadow side, and working with it. The first part leads with the traditional image (the Wheel of Fortune, the Tower Struck by Lightning). The last ends on a question.
2. **Say "the tradition".** The readings are Cheiro's, as modern editors summarize them. Write "the tradition reads it as" and "the tradition links it to", never "this means".
3. **Translate the fear.** The old readings talk of fatality, ruin and betrayal. Keep the image's name, and write the pattern in neutral terms: what a person may do, what it costs, what helps. No curses, no forecast, no "unlucky".
4. **An echo says it is an echo.** Where Cheiro gives a number no image of its own, the first sentence says it reads like the earlier number. Do not invent a separate image to fill the gap, and write each echo from what its two digits add.
5. **Masters are not masters here.** Say that 11, 22 and 33 are compounds in this tradition, and that modern Pythagorean writers treat them as masters.
6. **Be honest about age.** The name points to Babylon, but no ancient text sets the system out; the version in use is from the early 1900s.

### The shadows, in detail

1. **Eleven parts, always in this order:** where it comes from, how it sounds inside, at work, with people close to you, with time and energy, under pressure, early signals, what feeds it, what loosens it, the gift inside it, and a seven-day practice.
2. **Protects first, costs second.** A shadow began as a way of staying safe. Say what it protects before what it costs, so the reader recognizes it without feeling accused.
3. **Use the person's own voice for "how it sounds inside":** short quoted phrases ("I'll do it myself"), followed by a quieter line underneath. Not a diagnosis.
4. **Specific, with a cost and a benefit.** A concrete habit at work, a concrete habit at home. The gift is real, and it is the same trait turned around.
5. **Early signals are small and observable.** A sentence finished for someone, a message rewritten twice. Noticing one is enough; say that, and do not frighten.
6. **The practice is seven days, small, and ends on a question.** One thing each day, written so it can be done in five minutes.
7. **Use "early signals", not "warning signs"**, and keep to the lint's banned words, including will, never, always, must, leave and verdict, which turn up easily in this kind of writing.
